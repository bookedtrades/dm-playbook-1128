DM Outbound Playbook

Your job is to start conversations in DMs and turn them into booked calls with Will. This is a framework, not a script. Know the goal of each step, then write it in your own words using these examples.

Open→Goal→Gap→Bridge→Hook→Book + feed proof
Golden rules
  1. You're Will. Introduce yourself as Will from Booked Trades in every DM. It keeps things simple, since Will takes the call.
  2. Never end on a statement. Every message finishes with a question. If you make a point, send the question as its own text straight after.
  3. One question per message. Ask one thing, then wait for the reply. Never stack two asks in one bubble.
  4. Acknowledge, then ask. "Gotcha", "Totally understand", "Cool", then the next question. Use their own words back to them.
  5. Easy questions only. Short questions they can answer in a few words from the job site.
  6. Short and casual. Like a text to a mate, not an email. One or two lines per message.
  7. The gap matters most. If they never admit a gap between where they are and where they want to be, there's no reason to get on a call.
  8. No price in DMs. We haven't built the value yet.
  9. You drive. They answer, you steer to the next step.
  10. Always aim for the booking, then feed proof straight away.
  11. Say "home improvement and construction businesses" when describing who we work with, not "trades".
  12. Don't fake the slang. Casual and friendly is good. Forced "mate" and "legend" is not.
The thinking

Why We Do It This Way

The best calls are ones where the person shows up already knowing what we do, how we do it, roughly what it costs, and that it's worked for someone like them. Then the call is all about their business, not about convincing them. Your DMs are the first step in getting them there: find their goal and gap, book the call, then feed them proof that matches what they told you. Every case study, Instagram post and doc they read before the call makes it easier.

All the way through

Build Rapport

People book calls with people they like. Sound like a real person who's interested in their business, not a bot working a script. Every step below has rapport lines built in. These are the habits behind them.

The habits

  • Introduce yourself as Will. A name and a "nice to meet you" makes it a conversation, not a sequence.
  • Mention what they did. The guide they grabbed, the form they filled in, the job they posted. It shows you're paying attention.
  • Back them up after every answer. A quick "solid goal man" or "nice, good base to build off" before the next question.
  • Normalise their problems. "Super common", "all part of the growing pains". Nobody likes feeling like they're doing it wrong.
  • Soften your questions. "Out of curiosity", "quick one", "for context".
  • Match their energy. Short replies get short messages. If they open up, you can too.
  • Emoji sparingly. A 👍 here and there is friendly. More than that looks like spam.

Step 0 · Before any outbound

Postcode Check

Run every prospect's postcode through the outbound tool first. We only work with one business per service per area, so once a client has an area we don't prospect their competitors.

If there's a clash

Waitlist

If they've come to us anyway, it's not a bad thing. Explain the one-per-area model, send something helpful, and offer the waitlist.

Happy to send through some info on what you'd want to have in place either way. And if the spot ever opens up, want me to pop you on the waitlist?"

Step 1 · Open

Pick Your Opener

The goal is a quick reply and a conversation started. Pick the opener that fits the lead.

New Enquiry

Came through a funnel

Introduce yourself, mention what they grabbed, then ask about the work. Match it to the funnel they came through.

"Hey [name], my name's Will, nice to meet you 👍 Saw you grabbed [the guide] earlier. Are you a concreter, or what's your trade?"

"Hey, just got your inquiry, thanks for that! Curious, what sort of concreting work do you guys do?"

"Hey, just got your inquiry, thanks for that! What sort of work are you looking for exactly?"

Cold Outreach

We found them

Open on something real from their page, then a yes/no question.

"G'day [name], saw that [job / pour / deck] you posted, looks mint. Quick one, are you guys chasing more work at the moment, or pretty well booked up?"

Keep the rhythm going

New leads

They'll usually reply short. Clarify what they're after, find where they are, then tell them we have availability. The availability line is a statement, so the goal question goes straight after as its own text.

"Gotcha, totally understand. Whereabouts are you guys based?"

"Awesome. We're working with concreters across Aus at the moment, but we actually have availability in your area."

"So what's the goal for you guys at the moment?"

Step 1 · Open

Reactivation Openers

For old leads, quiet leads and anyone sitting in nurture. Lead with a result or a reason, finish with one easy question. Never a naked "just checking in".

Booked Out

Social proof

Best for leads who went cold on "not sure it works". Shows other clients are flat out.

"Hey [name], we just had 3 more clients pause their ads because they're booked out 12 weeks! Thought I'd see how you guys are going for work, have you got capacity to take on more at the moment?"

Spot Open

Area scarcity

Best for leads who were keen but the timing was off. One client per area makes it real.

"Quick one [name], how did you go with getting more consistent work? Thought I'd follow up as we've currently got an opening in [area]!"

Similar Win

Matched case

Best when you've got a case that mirrors their business. Swap the trade, city and number to match.

"[name], how's it going man! We just got one of our concreters in Melbourne over $250k in won jobs in two months! His business is actually pretty similar to yours, so I thought I'd reach out and see if you were still looking for more work too?"

Season Ahead

Timing

Best before a busy season or the new year. Gets them thinking about the pipeline, not today.

"G'day [name], most of the guys we work with are locking in their work for [summer / the new year] right now. How's the books looking for you over the next couple of months?"

Step 1 · Read the reply

Where They Land

Whatever they say back tells you which way to steer. Every path ends at the goal question.

Keen / bit quiet

"Could always use more" · "Bit slow at the moment"

"Righto, makes sense. So what's the goal for you guys at the moment?"

Booked up

"Flat out mate" · "Booked till Christmas"

"Love to hear it. Is that the kind of work you want more of, or more just whatever's been coming through?"

Busy isn't a no. It usually opens the expansion goal.

No reply

Seen, nothing back after 2 to 3 days

"No stress if you're flat out [name], just shout if more work's on the radar for [month]."

One bump, then see Follow-Up below.

Step 2 · Goal

Find Their Goal

Nearly every answer lands in one of two goals. Consistency is the most common. Expansion is our ideal client.

Ask the goal

Pick one

"So what's the goal for you guys at the moment?"

"Out of curiosity, what's the main thing you're trying to fix right now? Is it more about staying consistently booked, or getting better quality jobs coming through?"

Then dig into what they're aiming for:

"Yeah sweet. Have you guys got a goal you're shooting for this year?"

Most common

Consistency

Listen for: work is up and down, not working to capacity, feast or famine, word of mouth only.

Our ideal client

Expansion

Listen for: already busy and want to grow. Hiring more staff, a second team, or moving into a new niche, like commercial into residential.

If they say "both"

Second most common

They want to expand but don't have the consistency to get there yet. Consistency comes first, then confirm the expansion goal.

"Haha cool. Obviously consistency comes first, but once we lock that in, you guys want to expand the business, right?"

If they want the right jobs

Treat as expansion

Busy, but want better work: bigger jobs, fewer tyre kickers, or a specific type of job.

"What type of jobs do you actually want more of?"

"Roughly how much of your work right now is that kind of job?"

Step 3 · Gap · The step that matters most

How Much Work, And What's Stopping Them

Once they've said the gap in their own words, they want it fixed and the bridge makes sense. Don't rush it. What they tell you here also decides which proof you send after booking.

A

How much work they need

"Obviously it can vary, but roughly how many jobs are you guys doing a month at the moment? And how many would you like to be doing?"

If they say "it depends on the size of the job", agree and give them an example to react to:

"Of course it does. Just as a rule of thumb though, if we could bring in an extra say 4 of the right jobs a month in the beginning, would that help you guys? Or are you looking for more than that? We can help either way, just so I understand."

You want them to name the gap, e.g. "honestly an extra 1 to 2 jobs a week would do us". Back them up before you move on:

"Solid goal man, it's way easier to really scale things up from there too."

If they gave a revenue goal instead of jobs, ask where they're at now, then back that up too:

"For context, where are you sitting around now?"

"Nice man, that's a good base to build off. So what do you think the business needs most right now to hit [their goal]? More or better leads? More time for sales?"

B

What's stopping them

"Cool, so does most of your work come from word of mouth at the moment?"

"Have you been advertising at all?"

"What's stopping you getting there at the moment? Is it more on the sales side, or just not enough qualified leads coming your way?"

"Are you getting leads coming in but just too short on time to chase them?"

Common gaps: relying on word of mouth, not enough qualified leads, no time to follow up, trouble converting quotes, tried ads before and it didn't work.

The Wedge

Backup questions

Use these if their answer is vague. Let the answer sit. This is usually where the money leaks.

"And when an enquiry comes in, who chases it up, and how quick?"

"Are you working with anyone on that at the moment? How's it going?"

The Surface-Level Reframe

When they say "more leads"

Most guys think the problem is leads. Show them it usually runs deeper, then ask a question they'll say yes to.

"Yeah got it. Most of the time the lead problem is just surface level. It's usually a gap somewhere between enquiry, first conversation, quote and following up."

"Have you noticed the quoting slows down when work gets busy?"

Step 4 · Bridge

Show them their gap is normal and is exactly what we fix. Link their specific gap to the part of our service that solves it.

Step 5 · Hook

Tie It Back To Their Goal

Formula: "If we could [achieve their goal] and [remove their gap], is that something you'd be keen to look at?" Use their own words. If they say yes, go straight to booking.

Consistency

"If we could get a steady flow of qualified quotes coming in every week and take the chasing, follow-ups and time wasters off your plate, is that something you'd want to look at?"

New niche

"If we could help you get into that residential concreting space you're after, and take a whole lot of work off your plate in the meantime, is that something you'd be keen to look at?"

Second team

"If we could help you grow that second team by bringing in enough qualified quotes that you've got the certainty to support the new guys, would that help you get there quicker, do you think?"

Step 6 · Book it

Doc, Then Call

Move them off text and toward a call with Will. Tell them about the doc, then lock in a time. Offer two specific times, never "whenever suits".

1

Pitch the doc and the call

"Sweet. Look, I don't mind texting, but best thing from here is to send you a doc that lays out exactly how we do this. It's got examples of what we're doing for partners with really similar businesses to yours, and gives you an idea of pricing depending on what you need."

"I'll send that through for a read, then we'll jump on a quick call. Does [day] at [time] or [day] at [time] suit better?"

2

If they stall

"All good, what's your week looking like? Mornings before site or arvo after knock off are usually easiest."

Still vague? Lock a callback time instead and send the doc anyway.

3

Send the doc, stress reading it

"Here's that doc: [pre-call doc link]. Super important you give it a read before the call. It means we can spend the time diving into your business and how we'd tailor things to you, rather than going over our side of things."

4

Feed proof, straight away

"Locked in for [day]. Before then, have a look at this, a [trade] who was right where you're at and did [result]. That's the kind of thing we're talking about."

Send it the second the convo ends. One case matched to what they told you, not a dump. Use the table below.

5

Let them know who's calling

"Perfect, I'll give you a ring then. I'll flick you a text from my phone beforehand so you know who's calling."

Tradies don't answer unknown numbers on site. A heads-up text means the call actually connects.

Off script

Price And Objections

An objection isn't a no, it's just the next problem to solve. Find out what's really behind it, send something that answers that specific worry, then ask an easy question to keep moving. Keep it soft. Pushy closing lines come across badly in DMs.

"What's the price?"

Any time

Never give price in DMs. If they keep pushing, point to the doc, which gives an idea of pricing, and move to booking.

"Can definitely discuss price, just need to know exactly what you guys need first so we can get some clarity on it. What sort of work are you looking for more of?"
